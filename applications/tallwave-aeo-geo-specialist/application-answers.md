# Tallwave — Application Answers

## Describe one thing you have built in the AI Visibility domain

I built a 9-dimension audit for my consulting business that scores how likely a site is to be understood, trusted, and cited by search engines and AI assistants: entity clarity, topical coverage, content cluster architecture, internal linking, schema, information gain, query network coverage, trust and authority signals, and GEO/AI visibility. It runs as an automated workflow: a business enters its site, the workflow pulls and scores each dimension with custom logic and LLM calls, and the output triggers a personalized report and follow-up. I wrote rubric-based grading criteria for each dimension and hand-labeled outputs to check the scoring against my own judgment, then tightened the rubric where they disagreed. More than 180 businesses went through it, and two became paying clients. The honest limit: it measures the site-side inputs, not the answer-side outcome. The next version I'd build is the piece your role describes, a fixed prompt set sampled repeatedly across assistants, so the audit predicts and the prompt sampling proves.

## Brand shows up for category prompts but not purchase-intent prompts; a smaller competitor does. One week, no engineering time. What do you do first, and what would tell you you were wrong?

First I'd measure before touching anything. I'd build a small prompt set split by intent (category questions vs. "which product for this specific need"), run each prompt several times across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and AI Mode, and record presence, selection, and which sources get cited. The key question is what the assistant cites when it picks the competitor: their product and use-case pages, comparison articles, reviews, Reddit threads, or retailer listings.

My working hypothesis is that the brand has awareness content but not need-specific, passage-level answers, while the competitor has clear use-case pages, specs, and third-party comparisons that match the purchase prompts. With no engineering time, I'd act where marketing has access: rewrite or add need-specific sections and FAQs in the CMS, fix product data on retailer pages, and pursue the third-party sources the assistants are already citing. I'd also check robots.txt for blocked retrieval bots, since that's fast and changes everything if it's wrong.

I'd know I was wrong if the competitor's citations come mostly from sources the brand's own content can't influence, if the brand's pages are being retrieved but still not selected (a product-fit or positioning problem, not a content gap), or if a control prompt set moves as much as the target prompts, meaning the platform shifted, not our work. And I'd say plainly that one week is enough to diagnose and ship, not enough to prove lift.
