## Managing multiple client projects

Everything lives in one system, never in my head. Every client has its timeline, tasks, deliverables, and decisions documented in the project tool (I've used Asana, ClickUp, and Notion alongside the CRM), so at any point I can see what's due, who owns it, and what's blocked. At Wix I ran up to 25 client launches at once on fixed schedules, and the habit that made that work was checking dependencies early in the week, not on the due date.

When two deadlines land on the same day, I decide based on what's actually locked: a launch date a client has committed to their own customers beats an internal review date. Then I tell everyone affected right away, not at 5pm, with the new timing for whatever moves. Most conflicts get solved by spotting them two days earlier, which is why I look at the week ahead every Monday.

## Coordinating deliverables across a team

At Wix I led onboarding teams of up to 30 specialists launching 25+ client sites a month, so coordinating people toward fixed launch dates was the job. Each launch had pieces from different people, and my process was the same every time: clear owner and due date for each piece, a check-in before the deadline rather than on it, and a final review before anything reached the client. At Level Agency I coordinated campaign execution across internal departments the same way.

When someone fell behind, the first thing I did was find out why, because the fix is different if they're overloaded, blocked on someone else, or unclear on what's needed. If they were blocked, I cleared the blocker. If they were overloaded, I re-sequenced or moved work so the launch date held. And if the date truly couldn't hold, I told the client early with a new date, instead of letting them find out on launch day.
