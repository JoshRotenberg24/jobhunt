# Loom Script — Jamie's Live Welcome Call (target 5:00–5:30, hard cap 6:00)

Full verbatim script. Read it aloud twice before recording so it stops sounding like a script. Bracketed lines are stage directions, not spoken. Where a line implies Jamie answered, pause for a beat like you actually heard him before continuing, this is what makes a one-sided phone call read as real on camera.

## Setup (0:00–0:10)

[To camera, before going into character]

> This is Jamie's Welcome Call, about fifteen minutes after he signed up for Starter.

[Go straight into character. Don't break again until Beat 5.]

## Beat 1: Warm open (0:10–1:00)

> Hey Jamie, it's Josh calling from Starter, thanks for picking up. Congrats on getting started, how are you doing today?

[pause]

> Good, good. So here's how this call works. It's about ten minutes, and by the end you're fully logged in and set up for the rest of the program. Nothing you need to prep, I'll walk you through everything.

[pause]

> And hey, if tech's not really your thing, don't worry about it at all. That's exactly what I'm here for. We'll take it one step at a time.

## Beat 2: Solve the login live (1:00–2:15)

> So first thing, let's get you into the platform. You should have gotten a welcome email with your login link. Can you pull up your inbox for me?

[pause]

> No sign of it in the main inbox, that's alright, it happens more than you'd think. Try searching "Starter" right in the search bar.

[pause]

> Still nothing? Okay, check your spam or promotions folder, it sometimes lands there depending on your email provider.

[pause]

> There it is. Go ahead and open that one up... and click the link right in the middle, that's your account.

[pause]

> Perfect, you're in. Now before we do anything else, bookmark that page right now, right where you are. That way you've always got it, no more digging through your inbox.

[pause]

> Great, you're all set.

## Beat 3: Explain what's next, plain language (2:15–3:15)

> Okay, let me tell you exactly what happens from here, so none of this feels like a mystery.

> There are two calls total. This one, the Welcome Call, is just about getting you oriented. Later on there's a longer Onboarding Call, that's where we actually build out your plan.

> Before we wrap up today, there's a short questionnaire on the platform, maybe five minutes, it just tells us where you're starting from so the next call is useful instead of generic.

> And before that second call, the Onboarding one, there are three short exercises you'll do on your own time. They're called Audience, Problem, and Solution. I'm naming them now so when you see them later, they're familiar, not a surprise.

## Beat 4: Move to action (3:15–4:15)

> Let's knock out that questionnaire together right now, while I've got you on the phone, that way it's one less thing sitting on your to-do list.

[pause]

> ...Nice, that's done.

> Now let's get your Onboarding Call on the calendar so you're not thinking about it later. I've got Thursday at ten in the morning, or Monday at two in the afternoon, which works better for you?

[pause]

> Thursday at ten it is, you're booked.

> Alright Jamie, here's where you stand. You're logged in, your questionnaire's done, and your next call is locked in for Thursday at ten. If anything comes up before then, you've got my number, call or text me directly and I'll pick it up.

[pause]

> Sounds good. Talk soon, Jamie.

## Beat 5: Break character, explain the no-answer path (4:15–5:30)

[Step out of the roleplay, speak straight to camera]

> Now, if Jamie hadn't picked up at all, here's exactly what I'd do instead.

> First, I'm not waiting. Within the thirty minute window, if the call goes unanswered, I immediately send an SMS and an email with the Welcome Call booking link, that's the rule, get the link in front of him on two channels right away.

> In that same email, I'm also resending his platform login, since Jamie already flagged that as the thing blocking him before I even got him on the phone. No reason to make him ask for it twice.

> Then I log the attempt. Time of the call, channel, outcome, no answer, link sent, login resent. That's not optional, that's what lets the next person picking up this account know exactly where things stand without having to guess.

> And last, I set a follow up call for the same time tomorrow. That goes straight into my task list, not something I'm trusting myself to remember at the end of a busy shift.

> That's the whole loop. Thanks for watching.

## Delivery notes

- Stay fully in character through Beats 1–4. Don't explain the rules while roleplaying, that's what Beat 5 is for.
- The bracketed pauses matter, a real one-sided call has gaps where you're listening. Don't rush through them.
- If you're running long, trim Beat 2's back-and-forth (skip straight to "there it is, click that link") rather than shortening Beat 5, the no-answer explanation is what's actually being graded in the back half.
- Record in one take. If a line comes out wrong, keep going rather than restarting, the brief calls for unedited.
