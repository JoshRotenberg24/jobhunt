A client came to me convinced their problem was ad spend: not enough leads, so more budget was the answer. Before agreeing to scale anything, I pulled the Google Ads account data myself. Cost-per-click and impression share were fine, but a real share of the leads coming through were spam or clearly unqualified, and click-through quality was dragging the whole account down. I fixed that first: tightened targeting and messaging to cut the junk traffic and improve click-through quality. Lead quality got cleaner, but conversion still wasn't moving the way it should have.

That's when the data pointed somewhere else. Once the ad-side noise was gone, the numbers made it obvious the real bottleneck had moved to the site: people were clicking through fine, they just weren't converting once they landed. I brought that back to the client with the account data in hand and reframed the conversation from "we need more ad spend" to "we need to fix the page these ads are sending people to." The next quarter's priority shifted from scaling budget to conversion rate optimization on the landing page itself, which is where the actual leak was.

The part I'd want a hiring manager to take from this: the instinct to keep reading the data past the first fix, not stop at "the ads look better now," is what actually changes a client's strategy. Anyone can tighten an ad account. Recognizing when the bottleneck has moved somewhere else, and being willing to tell a client their initial ask was pointed at the wrong problem, is the harder and more useful skill.
