# Application Answers — Onboarding Manager, EULER

EULER's application asks for a cover letter built around four specific questions. The
rendered cover letter (`cover-letter.pdf` / `.docx`, also saved as `Rotenberg Cover
Letter - EULER - Onboarding Manager.pdf/.docx`) answers them in that order as one letter.
This file pairs each literal question with its answer for easy copy-paste if the portal
wants them in separate text boxes instead of an uploaded file.

## Describe an onboarding you owned from contract signature to go-live. What made the customer reach first value quickly?

At Solenzo, I own onboarding end to end for every client, from the signed agreement
through their first working deliverable. I sequence Google Business Profile setup first,
before any automation, because it is the one piece of work a client can verify himself, a
live, complete listing, without needing me to explain anything. That sequencing is the
standard opening move for every engagement today, not a one-off choice.

## Have you designed or redesigned an onboarding workflow or playbook from scratch? What did you change, and what was the impact?

At Wix, I built the onboarding process for a brand-new vertical from a blank page. It was
still proof of concept, so I started by defining the CRM fields the team needed for
onboarding and outbound sales, standardizing data capture before we scaled headcount,
then used that structure to build new-hire training and sales training for tenured
account executives. The vertical went from proof of concept to a profitable line, run
consistently instead of ad hoc.

## Describe a time an onboarding was at risk of stalling or failing. What did you catch early, and how did you get it back on track?

At Birdeye, regular utilization reviews across the 15 to 20 reseller partners I managed,
not a complaint, caught it: several partners had been onboarded and trained, but adoption
had quietly stalled after launch. I went back to them directly, found where the process
had broken down, and rebuilt the training and communication cadence that had let them go
quiet. That habit is a real part of why I retained all but one partner (about 94%) over
my tenure.

## Describe a handoff between Sales and Onboarding, or Onboarding and Customer Success, that you helped make smoother. What changed?

At Accelo, I built the sequenced implementation plan for an incoming client before the
deal closed, ranking which systems needed to change and in what order. That ranked plan
became the handoff I gave the implementation team, so they inherited a decision already
made about where the risk sat instead of re-interviewing the client from scratch.
Implementation started executing on day one instead of losing a week to rediscovery.
