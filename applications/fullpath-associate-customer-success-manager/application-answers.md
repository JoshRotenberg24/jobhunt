## What's the most impressive thing you've built, done, or contributed to that had real-world impact?

At Wix I was part of the team that took a brand-new onboarding vertical from proof of concept to a profitable business line. I started as an account manager launching small businesses onto the platform and became team lead, running onboarding teams of up to 30. The problem was that nothing about the vertical was proven yet: not the process, not the team structure, not the handoffs. I pitched and led process changes as we tested different team setups, helped hire and train new specialists, and built sales training for tenured account executives. Personally I launched 25+ clients a month at 120% of sales and 110% of implementation targets, and across the e-commerce segment we supported, traffic rose 30% and conversion rates improved 25%. My manager wrote that she'd "hire him again in an instant."

## What's a challenge you took on that wasn't required, but you did anyway?

Small business owners kept asking me why they didn't show up on Google or in AI tools like ChatGPT, and most had no idea what an SEO audit even meant. Nobody was paying me to fix that, so I built a free, automated audit that scores a business's online presence across nine dimensions, and wrote 18 articles explaining each piece in plain language for non-technical owners. I distributed it through my own LinkedIn. More than 180 businesses enrolled in the audit and two became paying clients. The bigger result is that it forced me to turn what I knew into a clear, repeatable framework, which is now how I run every audit.

## What's something you've taught yourself that helped you become more effective in your role?

I taught myself to build integrations directly instead of stopping where no-code tools stop: webhooks, REST API calls, LLM APIs, and reading JSON payloads, plus Python and JavaScript in automation code steps. I learned by building real client workflows and using AI tools to explain errors and documentation as I went. It changed what I can deliver on my own. I can trace where a data sync breaks, fix it, and automate the repetitive parts of a workflow without waiting on an engineer.
