My role across the lifecycle has been the same: own discovery and the plan, coordinate the people doing the build, train the customer, and watch adoption after launch.

## Quarterbacking a complex implementation

At Accelo, the implementations I planned touched CRM, project management, time tracking, and billing at once, and every module fed the next, so a decision in one changed how the others worked. I acted as the bridge between the customer and our implementation team. I mapped how each firm actually ran, built a sequenced plan deciding which systems to change and in what order so the dependencies were explicit, and handed it to the delivery team with clear ownership for each piece. When I saw a risk to the plan, I escalated it to leadership early rather than waiting for it to show up as a missed date, and I kept the customer's executives informed so nothing surprised them. The same discipline carried into Wix, where I ran up to 25 launches at once: every launch had an owner, a date, and a check-in before the deadline, not on it.

## Misaligned expectations

At Wix, many clients came in expecting to launch on the first call, and for simpler sites that was the goal I worked toward. For more complex builds, that expectation didn't match what the work actually required. Rather than rushing a launch that would break, I reset the expectation directly: I explained what the build involved, why it needed more than one session, and laid out a planned production schedule across multiple sessions with what would be done at each one. Internally, I made sure the team knew the revised scope so nobody was working against the old assumption. Clients accepted it because they could see exactly what they were getting and when, and the launch landed on the new date instead of slipping without warning.

## Measuring and improving onboarding success

At Birdeye, the metric I watched most closely was partner utilization. Usage almost always dropped before a partner complained, so I tracked it across my 15-20 partners, spotted the ones falling off early, and stepped in with retraining and automated product-training sequences that got their own teams up to speed without depending on me. Over my tenure I retained all but one partner, about 94%. At Wix, I measured myself against implementation targets and launch timelines, holding 110% of my implementation target while launching 25+ clients a month, and the e-commerce segment I supported saw a 25% improvement in conversion rates.
