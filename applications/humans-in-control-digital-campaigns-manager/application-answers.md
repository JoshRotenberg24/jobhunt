## 6. One argument for AI rules that lands with conservatives but not progressives, and vice versa

**Lands with conservatives, falls flat with progressives:** "No company gets to write its own rules. A family business has to follow the law; a handful of tech billionaires shouldn't get a pass. And parents, not a chatbot company, should decide what their kids are exposed to." Accountability for the powerful and parental authority land well. With progressives, "parental rights" reads as culture-war code, and "rules for everyone equally" can sound like it's dodging who gets hurt most.

**Lands with progressives, falls flat with conservatives:** "AI is being sold to employers as a cheaper way to replace workers, and the gains go to a few companies while working people carry the cost. We need protections before the layoffs, not after." Worker protection and concentrated corporate power land well. Conservatives hear it as class conflict and a call for more federal bureaucracy.

**The craft point:** both audiences agree that nobody should grade their own homework. I'd lead with that shared frame and use the audience-specific proof (a deepfake of a local teenager, a data center the town voted against) as the example, not the argument.
