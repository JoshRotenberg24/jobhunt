## Implementation or onboarding project, kickoff to completion

At Birdeye I owned onboarding for a reseller channel of 15 to 20 marketing agencies, including several focused on healthcare clients. My role was the partner's main contact from signing through go-live: I trained each agency's team on Birdeye's reputation and marketing automation tools, guided them through technical setup, and made sure every one of their end customers was configured on our listings solution, which was the backbone of the offer. That meant working with the agency owners, their account teams, and indirectly their own clients, so I built automated training sequences and communication workflows to keep everyone moving without things slipping. The outcome: all but one partner stayed with us through my tenure (about 94% retention), on deals ranging from roughly $10K to $45K.

## Managing competing priorities in a high-volume pipeline

At Wix I carried up to 25 onboardings at once and launched 25+ clients a month, while also leading the onboarding team and, in my second year, carrying a sales target. I held 110% of my implementation target and 120% of my sales target.

What kept it organized was structure, not heroics. I defined the Salesforce fields our onboarding workflow needed, so every account's status and next step lived in one place and nothing depended on memory. I aimed to launch simple builds on the first call and scoped complex ones into planned multi-session production up front, so customers knew exactly what to expect and when. Today at Solenzo I go further: automated confirmations, reminders, and follow-ups handle the routine touches, and every configuration is documented in Notion, so my time goes to the customers who actually need a conversation.
