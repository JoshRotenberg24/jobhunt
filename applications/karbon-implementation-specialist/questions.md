## "Briefly share a time you led a complex project from a new client's initial onboarding to a successful launch."
*Drafted August 6, 2026*

**Honesty notes before the answer:** the master profile records the Wix onboarding role
and its results at the segment level, not a single named client. This is therefore
written as the pattern across the complex launches he ran, stated plainly as a pattern,
with the +30% traffic and +25% conversion figures kept at the segment scope where they
are true. If Josh can name a specific account and supply its numbers, swapping it in
makes this materially stronger.

> At Wix I led onboarding and launch for high-value e-commerce accounts, and I also ran
> the team of onboarding specialists doing that work.
>
> The complex ones followed the same shape. Kickoff to understand what the business
> actually sold and how it operated day to day, then a build plan with clear milestones
> and named owners on both sides. Four things decided whether a launch worked: site
> architecture, product data and catalog structure, SEO foundations, and conversion
> paths. Most of the risk sat in the data, so I front-loaded it. Getting a catalog
> structured correctly before build is a fraction of the cost of fixing it after launch.
>
> The other half was change management. These were owners who had run their business a
> certain way for years, so training had to cover the new process, not just where to
> click.
>
> Across the e-commerce segment I supported, that work contributed to a 30% increase in
> traffic and a 25% improvement in conversion rates. The launch playbook we built became
> what we trained new specialists on.

**Why it's built this way:** opens with scope (led the work and the team), names one real
decision with its reasoning (front-loading data) rather than describing a generic
process, and spends a paragraph on change management because Karbon lists it as a bonus
criterion where Josh has practical proof but no framework certification.

**Upgrade available:** a named account, its launch timeline, and the number of records in
its catalog migration. All three are on the profile's *Numbers Worth Capturing* list.
