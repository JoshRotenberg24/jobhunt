# Sprouts Farmers Market Interview Prep — Administrative Coordinator

**Store:** Westminster, CO (#0301) · **Req:** 376084 · **Score:** 61/100, Solid stretch

---

## Who's likely interviewing and what they're screening for

This is a store-level hourly req, not a corporate one. The interview is almost always the **Store Director or Assistant Store Manager**, sometimes with HR/People support in the room, 20 to 30 minutes, structured behavioral questions plus a few operational specifics. They are not testing SaaS/sales pedigree. They're testing three things:

1. **Can this person be trusted with cash, personnel files, and payroll data day one** (the core of the job).
2. **Will this person actually stay**, given the resume reads as a clear downward move from a career in SaaS sales and founder work.
3. **Do they fit a fast, people-facing, retail floor environment** — not a remote desk job.

Answer every question with that lens: concrete, short, evidence-first. Retail managers interview a lot of people and tune out rehearsed corporate language fast.

---

## The question they will ask, one way or another: "why this?"

This is the single highest-probability question, and the one Josh is most likely to fumble if he over-explains. The honest answer, confirmed by Josh: **the job market is bad right now**. Say that plainly, don't dress it up as a passion pivot, and pair it with what's actually true alongside it:

> "Straight answer: the market for the kind of SaaS role I've spent my career in has been rough this year, and I wanted something local, stable, and in person while that plays out. I'm not looking to fake enthusiasm for a career change I don't have. What I do have is fifteen years of the actual work this role needs: hiring and onboarding people, keeping records straight, pulling reports on a deadline, handling cash accurately. I'd rather be useful here than idle waiting for the SaaS market to loosen up."

If pushed on "will you leave the second something else comes up": don't overpromise a made-up long-term plan. "I'm looking for something real right now, not a placeholder. If I take this, I'm taking it seriously" is honest and defensible without a fabricated five-year story.

## Core evidence to lead with (in order of relevance to this specific req)

**1. Cash handling — now a real, direct answer, not a dodge.**
"I co-owned and ran operations for a licensed business early in my career where, because of limited banking access at the time, I personally counted cash and handled till and deposit activity as a routine, daily part of running the place. That's real hands-on cash-handling experience, not adjacent to it." *(Standing Akimbo, 2010–2012, confirmed by Josh Sep 2026.)* Be ready for a follow-up on specifics: it was a cash-heavy licensed cannabis dispensary during Colorado's early medical-marijuana era, not a grocery POS system, so be straight that the *system* (till, receipts, formal safe-balancing procedure) will be new even though the underlying discipline (accuracy, counting, reconciling, not being casual with other people's money) isn't.

**2. Hiring, onboarding, training — the strongest, most literal match in the whole profile.**
"At Wix I directly managed and developed a team of onboarding specialists for two years, twenty people, then eight, and was part of building that whole function from scratch, including hiring and training new hires. My manager there put that in writing in a recommendation, unprompted." Use this for any people/HR-support question — new-hire paperwork, training materials, conflict resolution.

**3. Reports, records, deadline accuracy.**
"Every client record I keep at my own company has to be auditable, on deadline, across several accounts running at once. Before that, at Level Agency and Fetch & Funnel, I built and fixed reporting processes specifically because handoff errors were a real risk." Maps directly onto "pull and distribute sales/labor reports" and "keep store files organized."

**4. Microsoft Office — confirmed, don't hedge.**
Real, hands-on experience with Outlook, Excel, and Word, not just Google Workspace. Say it plainly if asked; no need to qualify it.

**5. Schedule flexibility.**
Nights, weekends, and holidays are genuinely fine. Say so without hesitation, this removes a real disqualifier for a lot of candidates and costs nothing to confirm directly.

## Where the honest gaps are, and how to answer if asked directly

**"Have you worked in a grocery or retail store before?"**
No, be straight about it. "Not grocery specifically. The closest is running operations for a small licensed retail business early on, cash handling, compliance, customer and vendor systems, but not a store like this one. What I'd bring is fifteen years of the surrounding skills, plus I learn operational systems fast, that's most of what my career has actually been."

**"Tell me about your experience with [specific till/POS system, safe balancing, WIC/coupon reconciliation]."**
He doesn't have this. Don't invent a system name or fake familiarity. "I haven't worked that specific process before. I have handled cash accurately and under real stakes, so I'm not starting from zero on judgment, just from zero on this particular system, and I pick up structured processes quickly."

**"Any bookkeeping or payroll software experience — ADP, Kronos, similar?"**
Real gap. "No hands-on time with a payroll or HRIS platform specifically. I've worked inside CRM and operational systems for years (Salesforce, HubSpot, GoHighLevel, Accelo), so structured data-entry systems with real consequences for accuracy aren't new to me, just not that particular category of tool."

**Don't manufacture confidence on either of these.** A retail store manager will spot a fabricated answer immediately, and it costs more trust than a clean "no, but here's what's adjacent."

## Likely behavioral/situational questions and how to answer them

Standard retail-management interview set. Use the **situation, action, result** shape, short, no throat-clearing.

- **"Tell me about a time you handled money or a sensitive record inaccurately, or almost did, and what you did about it."** Pull from Standing Akimbo cash handling or a records-accuracy moment at Solenzo/Level Agency. Own any real near-miss honestly if one exists, that answer lands better than a too-clean "never happened."
- **"Tell me about a time you dealt with an upset customer or coworker."** Draw from the Wix/Birdeye onboarding work, real conflict-resolution reps.
- **"Tell me about a time you had to keep something confidential."** Personnel/HR files are core to this role. If nothing employment-specific comes to mind live, it's fine to say so and pivot to the trust required in handling client and account data at Solenzo.
- **"How do you prioritize when everything feels urgent at once?"** Solenzo running multiple concurrent client engagements, or Wix's combined sales-and-implementation year two, are real, specific answers.
- **"Describe your availability."** Confirm nights/weekends/holidays directly, then give a real weekly availability window before the interview so this doesn't stall live.

## Logistics to have ready before the call

- **Start date.** Have a real answer, not "immediately" by default if that's not true.
- **Hourly wage expectation.** This is an hourly req, not salaried, standing Compensation Expectations baseline in `master-profile.md` doesn't apply directly. If asked, ask what the posted range is first rather than anchoring blind, then confirm a number with Josh before committing to one out loud.
- **Physical requirements confirmation.** Lifting ~30 lbs, extended standing, most likely a self-certification at application or onboarding, not something to over-prepare a story for, just confirm yes.

"Because the parts of this job that are hard to teach, being accurate with money, being trusted with confidential records, handling people and hiring well, I've already done for fifteen years. The part that's new is the specific system, and that's the fast part to learn."
