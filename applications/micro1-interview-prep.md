# micro1 Interview Prep — Both Roles

**Sales / Revenue / CS Operations Expert** (87/100, strong fit) and **Revenue Operations Specialist (SaaS)** (66/100, solid stretch). Same company, same AI-recruiter screening model, same underlying evidence. This doc preps both at once and flags where they diverge.

---

## What micro1 is actually testing

The scope of work on both postings says the deliverable is "process walk-throughs" and "scenario-based operational training tasks" with "decision points and lessons learned." That's not a normal interview asking what you did, it's asking you to narrate *why* you chose one path over another. Every answer should follow the same shape:

**Situation → what I chose → why → what I'd do differently or watch for next time.**

Skip the "why" and the answer is worthless to them, that's literally the training signal they're buying. Don't polish it into a clean success story. If there's a tradeoff or a thing you'd do differently, say it, that's more valuable to them than a win.

## Core talking points (both roles)

**CRM ownership, personal not delegated.**
"At Solenzo I architect CRM systems in GoHighLevel and HubSpot myself, contact segmentation, lifecycle stages, data-hygiene protocols. At Accelo I sold and implemented a product that's CRM, project tracking, and billing in one system, so I've had to think about data models that hold up across connected systems, not just a single CRM." Lead with this. It's your strongest, most literal match on both postings.

**Prioritizing what to change vs. what to leave stable.**
"When clients came in to implement Accelo, I built sequenced implementation plans ranking which systems to change first and which to leave alone. The logic: fix what's actively costing deals or creating errors first, leave what's working even if it's not elegant until there's a real reason to touch it. Changing things that aren't broken just to make them cleaner is risk without payoff." This is a near-exact match to the SaaS role's "prioritizing what to change versus what to leave stable" ask, use it there specifically if they probe.

**Escalating risk early.**
"I escalated issues to leadership early at Accelo rather than waiting for them to surface as client complaints. Waiting for permission to escalate is how small risks become account losses." Good general answer for "operational judgment" on either posting.

**Data integrity across systems.**
"Every client interaction, timeline, and deliverable gets documented across GoHighLevel, HubSpot, and Notion, records that stay auditable across concurrent engagements. At Level Agency I built reporting frameworks tracking ROI, CAC, LTV against conversion KPIs for QBRs." Use for "data accuracy and reporting."

**Why contractor work.**
"I run Solenzo, my own consulting practice, as a contractor engagement already. This isn't a new arrangement for me, I like scoped work with a clear deliverable." Kills any "is this person going to flake on a 1099 gig" concern in one line.

## Where the two roles diverge

### Role 1 (Sales/Revenue/CS Ops Expert) — lean in, this is your strongest one

No real gaps to manage defensively. If they ask about tools beyond the CRM, lead with Accelo (native billing/quoting/project tracking) and ClickUp (Fetch & Funnel). If they ask about driving adoption, use Birdeye: "I onboarded and managed 15 to 20 reseller partners across health, home services, and retail, retaining all but one, about 94 percent, by building communication workflows and automated training sequences that reduced onboarding friction."

### Role 2 (Revenue Ops Specialist SaaS) — two things to handle straight, not around

**"How do you handle two conflicting operational policies?"**
You don't have a clean example of literally arbitrating between two written policies. Don't invent one. Say:

"I don't have a case where I was choosing between two documented policies. What I've handled is closer to Accelo: an account moving in a direction that violated the intent of how the system was supposed to work, so I flagged it and proposed the fix instead of letting it become policy by accident. If this role means adjudicating between two formal rules, I'd want to understand the failure mode you're testing before I manufacture a scenario, that's a different exercise than what I've done. But the underlying judgment, protect the business first, escalate early, be able to explain the reasoning, is the same muscle."

That's honest, doesn't apologize, and redirects to real evidence. Don't soften it further.

**"Experience with fintech, healthtech, or insurtech?"**
"None directly. What I have is Standing Akimbo, a highly regulated startup I co-owned early in my career, where I built compliance and customer-management systems from scratch. That's real compliance-building experience, but it predates my SaaS career and wasn't a SaaS product. I'd rather tell you that straight than stretch it. What transfers is the discipline: building a system where the rules are non-negotiable and the workflow still has to work for the people using it."

Say this early if it comes up, don't wait for them to catch it.

## Rate conversation

If they ask your rate before you've asked anything: "What's the typical rate range for someone with this background on this specific project?" is a fair question to ask back first, it's information you're entitled to before naming a number.

If pushed to anchor first, here's the reasoned range, not a number pulled from your profile, so confirm it actually works for you before using it live:

- **Role 1 ($40–80/hr):** anchor around **$65–70/hr**. Top third of the band is justified, 15+ years, four-CRM breadth (HubSpot, Salesforce, GoHighLevel, Accelo) is a rare combination for this kind of gig.
- **Role 2 ($50–100/hr):** anchor around **$78–85/hr**. Upper-middle rather than top of band, the fintech/healthtech/insurtech gap is real and they'll likely have specialists in that band who don't have it.

If the number they float is below your anchor, don't just take it, ask "what's driving that range for this project" before you move. That tells you whether it's a hard budget ceiling or a starting offer.
