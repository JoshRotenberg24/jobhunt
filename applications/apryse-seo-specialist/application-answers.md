# Application Questions — Apryse, SEO Specialist

## Which areas of SEO do you consider your strongest (technical SEO, content strategy, on-page optimization, site audits), and what types of projects best reflect that strength?

Technical SEO and site audits are where I do my strongest work. At Solenzo, I run technical SEO audits with Screaming Frog across client sites, pulling site-wide data, flagging schema and structured data gaps, then implementing the fixes directly inside the client's CMS rather than just handing over a list. That full loop, audit to keyword gap analysis to competitor research to implementation, is the project type that reflects it best. On-page work follows from the same audits: metadata, headings, schema, internal linking.

Content strategy is where I'm less specialized. Most of my content work has been producing it myself, thought leadership and educational content, rather than writing formal SEO briefs for a team of writers to execute against.

## Describe a time you identified an organic search opportunity or problem that wasn't obvious from a dashboard alone. What data did you connect, and what action did it lead to?

At Solenzo I run Screaming Frog crawls alongside keyword gap and competitor research for each client, and the pattern that comes up most often isn't a ranking drop, it's pages that are indexable and ranking fine, but showing up without rich results because structured data is missing or broken. A dashboard shows traffic and rankings holding steady, it doesn't show that a competitor's listing has review or FAQ markup and yours doesn't. Connecting the crawl data to the competitor SERP itself is what surfaces that gap. The action is always the same: implement or fix the schema in the client's CMS, then recheck the SERP for the rich result. I'm describing this as a repeating finding across engagements rather than one story, since it shows up on most sites I audit that haven't had a technical SEO pass before.

## How familiar are you with AEO/GEO or AI-driven search, and what recent developments in search are you following most closely?

I haven't worked directly with AEO or GEO tools like citation tracking or AI share-of-voice platforms. What I do have is hands-on experience building the systems on the other side of that problem: architecting AI agent workflows, writing structured prompts, and translating loosely-defined business input into instructions an LLM can act on reliably. That gives me a practitioner's view of how these models parse and prioritize content, not just a marketer's view of the output.

The shift I'm watching most closely is how much weight structured, well-sourced, clearly-scoped content carries in AI-generated answers compared to traditional ranking factors. It's the same instinct behind the schema work I already do in technical SEO, applied to a newer surface.
