## Requirement coverage

**Covered (strong)**

- *Comfortable between executive strategy and hands-on execution* — Accelo C-suite advisory
  alongside hands-on workflow configuration; Solenzo is entirely both.
- *Cross-functional partnership across Product, Sales, CS, Marketing* — Birdeye fed
  structured feedback to Product and Engineering; Level Agency coordinated execution across
  internal departments; Wix spanned sales and implementation.
- *Thrives in a startup, drives strategy through execution* — founded and runs Solenzo;
  co-owned Standing Akimbo and built its operating systems from scratch.
- *Writes the messaging and the campaign assets personally* — landing pages, email
  sequences, direct-response copy, thought leadership, all his own hands.
- *Turns research into direction* — competitor research, keyword gap analysis, and
  performance analysis feeding positioning and campaign decisions.

**Covered (adjacent)**

- *Market development / opening a new segment* — the Wix vertical went from proof of concept
  to a profitable business line with Josh driving team initiatives and building the sales
  enablement behind it. This is the single best proof for the JD's "not just executing
  product launches" line, and it is a team contribution inside a large company rather than
  him owning a vertical GTM strategy end to end. Represent it that way.
- *Sales enablement with messaging and proof points* — built sales training for tenured AEs
  at Wix; equipped Birdeye resellers with positioning and health metrics to sell in their
  own markets. Enablement, yes; not a formal PMM enablement program.
- *Translating complex technology and data into simple narratives* — the core of Solenzo's
  work, with automation and performance data rather than clinical science.
- *Multi-channel campaign orchestration* — Level Agency, across inbound, paid, and content.

**Gaps (genuinely absent from the profile)**

- **A Product Marketing title or function.** No PMM role, no positioning doc, no launch
  he owned as the product marketer. The skills are adjacent; the function is not on the
  resume, and a 7+ years requirement is often screened literally.
- **Digital health / healthcare.** No clinical evidence, no medical device, no health
  system or payer buyer, no senior care, no regulated-health messaging.
- **Category creation at company scale.** The JD is explicit about building a new global
  category. Josh's closest analog is one vertical inside Wix.
- **Formal PMM artifacts** — ICP definitions, buyer journey maps, win/loss analysis,
  analyst relations, PR. None appear anywhere in the profile.

**Candidate confirmation needed (questions for Josh, not gaps)**

- **Any healthcare-adjacent client work at Solenzo or Level Agency** — a clinic, a dental
  or therapy practice, a home-care agency, a med-spa. Local service businesses often include
  these, and even one would change how the domain gap reads. It is not in the profile.
- **Whether any Level Agency account was in a regulated vertical** — Level does a lot of
  education and healthcare work. If one of his accounts was, that belongs in the profile.
- **Whether he has ever written positioning or a launch narrative for a product** (as
  opposed to for a client's service). Nothing in the profile says so either way.

## Recommendation

**Apply only if two things are true:** the role is remote-US friendly, and Josh genuinely
wants to spend the next stretch learning healthcare. On substance he is a long shot rather
than a bad fit — the reasoning and storytelling this JD wants are real strengths, but a
7+ year Product Marketing requirement paired with a digital-health category-building mandate
is two simultaneous stretches, and companies hiring a category-defining PMM usually screen
for the title.

If he does apply, the cover letter is essential rather than optional here, and it names both
gaps in the first paragraph rather than waiting to be caught on them. The Wix vertical
carries the argument.
