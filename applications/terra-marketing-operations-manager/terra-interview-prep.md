## The financial services question (prepare for it)

They'll likely ask about financial services or regulated-industry marketing. Be straight about what you have:

**What's real:**
- Fetch & Funnel had a **banking client** on its roster, and you were part of pursuing a **major northeastern U.S. bank**, where the agency made the finalist round.
- **Regulated industry:** you co-owned a **licensed cannabis dispensary** (Standing Akimbo) in Colorado's early medical-marijuana era and built its compliance systems from scratch. That's about as regulated as it gets. Also, Birdeye channel sales to agencies focused on **healthcare** clients.
- Genuine interest: you like the idea of making financial decisions and purchase processes easier for people and businesses.

**What's not:** no wealth management, RIA, or advisor-marketing experience. Don't imply it.

**Know the vocabulary** (prep knowledge, not a claim of experience):
- **Wirehouse:** a large national brokerage (Morgan Stanley, Merrill, UBS, Wells Fargo) where advisors are employees and marketing runs through heavy central compliance and approved templates.
- **Independent advisor:** an RIA or an advisor at an independent broker-dealer who owns their practice and brand, and markets it themselves, still under compliance rules.
- **Rules that govern the marketing:** FINRA Rule 2210 (communications with the public, with pre-approval for many materials) and the SEC Marketing Rule (testimonials, performance claims). In practice every email, social post, and landing page goes through compliance review, so campaign timelines have to build that in.

Suggested answer: "My direct financial services work is agency-side with a banking client and a large bank pursuit. The regulated-industry muscle comes from co-owning a licensed cannabis dispensary, where every customer interaction had a compliance layer. I know the advisor side works under FINRA and SEC marketing rules, so compliance review has to be built into the campaign calendar, not tacked on at the end."
