# Interview Prep — Duda, Senior Partner GTM Manager

**Date started:** 2026-08-26

**Comp range posted:** $150,000–$180,000 base + equity + benefits
**Note:** the same requisition (Greenhouse job 7747621003) also surfaces under the title "Senior Partner Marketing Manager" on Duda's own careers page and job boards — same role, two title variants. Worth knowing if you search Glassdoor/LinkedIn for prep material.

## 1. Company snapshot

- Founded 2009 by **Itai Sadan** (CEO) and Amir Glatt. Started in mobile-optimized websites, now a white-label website/app platform sold **through** agencies, SaaS companies, and web professionals — not directly to end businesses.
- Raised **$96M+ total**, most recently a **$50M Series D** (Claridge IL, Susquehanna Growth Equity, Vintage Investment Partners). Estimated **~$41M ARR** (2024 figure) and **~22,800 customers** (agencies/SaaS partners, not end sites).
- Colorado presence (Louisville, CO) is a real hub, not a satellite — the company has won multiple Colorado "Best Places to Work" awards, per the JD.
- **Core positioning: B2B2B2C.** Duda doesn't sell to the SMB — it sells the platform to agencies and vertical SaaS companies, who resell/embed it to their own SMB customers. This shapes almost everything about the role: the "customer" in most of your work is a partner's GTM team, not the SMB itself.

## 2. Partner ecosystem (this is the job)

- Historically Duda's partner base includes large media/directory companies reselling websites to local SMB advertisers — **DexYP, Hibu, Italiaonline, 1&1, UOL, Sensis, Publicar** are named publicly as agency partners. This is exactly the "large media agencies" language in the JD — it's not hypothetical, it's Duda's existing anchor accounts.
- Partner geography matches the JD: primary strength in **US and Europe**, with **growing LATAM and APAC** ecosystems. In 2025 Duda ran partner meetings across US/European markets to accelerate this growth.
- **Vertical SaaS** is the newer leg — SaaS platforms serving a specific vertical (property management, healthcare, home services, etc.) that embed website-building as a feature for their own SMB customers rather than build it themselves. Duda publishes a "Vertical SaaS strategy" point of view — worth skimming before the interview since this is explicitly called out as a growth pillar in the JD.

## 3. Product — what you'd be enabling partners to sell

Duda has shipped several AI-forward releases through 2026 that a partner GTM role would be expected to turn into partner-facing enablement and messaging:

- **Duda Vibe** (conversational/"vibe-coding" editor for launching custom web apps and business tools, not just sites)
- **Enhanced AI site generation** — agencies can upload briefs, transcripts, or documents alongside a prompt and get a matching site
- **AI-powered custom widget generator** — no-code, prompt-driven reusable widgets
- **Populate Template with AI**, AI-generated SEO metadata and alt text, tone/length/translation editing tools

The through-line the role needs to sell: **AI lets a partner's SMB customers get a higher-quality site faster, without the partner needing more production staff.** That's the packaging story — translate product capability into partner economics (their cost-to-serve, their upsell surface), not just feature lists.

## 4. Competitive context — including a personal wrinkle

Direct competitors: **Wix, Squarespace, WordPress/Elementor-type ecosystems, GoDaddy Website Builder**, and increasingly vertical-specific embedded builders.

**Josh worked at Wix (Aug 2020–Aug 2022)** as an Account Manager / Onboarding Launch Specialist / Team Lead — direct-to-SMB, not white-label. This is worth naming proactively rather than hoping it doesn't come up:

- It's a real asset: deep, hands-on knowledge of SMB website psychology, what makes an SMB churn or renew, onboarding friction points, and what "time-to-value" means for a small business owner. That's the exact end customer Duda's partners are trying to serve.
- It's also a legitimate contrast to draw out unprompted: Wix's model is direct-to-consumer; Duda's is B2B2B2C through a partner. Naming that distinction shows an understanding of *why* the GTM motion here is fundamentally different (enabling someone else's sales/marketing team, not running your own), rather than assuming the two are interchangeable.

## 6. Real gaps — name these honestly, don't oversell

- **No formal "product marketing" or "partner marketing" title.** The closest analog is Birdeye's channel-partnerships work plus Solenzo's asset-building — real, but the job titles won't pattern-match on a resume scan. Be ready to state this plainly and let the work speak for it, the way the Quilt and other match reports in this repo already do — don't inflate a title that wasn't held.
- **No large media agency or multi-national partner experience specifically.** Birdeye's partners were mid-market resellers, not the DexYP/Hibu/Italiaonline scale of media conglomerate. Say so directly if asked; pivot to the structural similarity (managing a partner's ability to resell to SMBs at volume) rather than claiming scale that wasn't there.
- **No evidenced large-scale event/summit ownership.** The JD calls out planning Duda's partner summit specifically. Nothing in the work history is a direct match (Wix team-lead work is the closest adjacent experience — hiring, training, process-building for a new vertical). If asked directly, be honest that this would be new ground, and pivot to the organizational/process-building instinct that's actually evidenced (Wix vertical build-out, Solenzo's systemization of client work).
- **No LATAM/APAC-specific experience.** Not evidenced anywhere in the work history. Don't claim it.
- **Vertical SaaS partner motion (as opposed to agency reseller motion) is the newer, less-evidenced half of this role.** Birdeye's partners were mostly agencies/resellers, not SaaS platforms embedding a product. Fine to note this is the side of the role most worth ramping into.

## 7. Likely interview themes and how to answer them

Use the **first-sentence-establishes-scope** rule from the master profile: name the scope before the story.

**"Tell me about a time you enabled a partner to sell more effectively."**
→ Birdeye: 15–20 resellers, $10K–$45K deals, cross-vertical. Name one specific intervention — e.g. the automated training/communication workflows built to cut onboarding friction — and the retention outcome (94%), not just the activity.

**"How have you used AI or automation in your own work?"**
→ This is a real strength, not a rehearsed line. Solenzo: 20+ GoHighLevel workflows spanning lead capture through review generation, an automated prospect-scoring system that enrolled 180+ contacts, and the specific skill of turning a non-technical stakeholder's vague ask into precise agent logic. Frame the last part as directly transferable: partner GTM teams hand over messy, half-formed asks too, and the job is the same translation exercise.

**"How would you build a scalable enablement program from scratch?"**
→ Pull from Solenzo's boilerplate-asset practice: build a reusable template/framework base first, then adapt per partner/vertical rather than bespoke per partner. Cite the discipline of documenting everything (GHL/HubSpot/Notion) as the reason programs stay auditable and repeatable across concurrent partners.

**"Tell me about working cross-functionally with sales/account teams to drive partner growth."**
→ Birdeye again — but also Wix, where cross-functional work extended to hiring and training for a brand-new vertical (corroborated by a direct manager's written recommendation, on file in the master profile). Naming that corroboration if pressed on evidence is legitimate — it's a real, attributed reference, not a claim standing alone.

**"Why Duda, why this role?"**
→ Genuine answer available here, not a generic one: direct SMB-side experience at Wix gives insight into the end customer Duda's partners serve, while Birdeye gives the partner-enablement motion itself. The AI-enablement angle in the JD is a real, evidenced strength rather than a buzzword match. Say this plainly rather than performing enthusiasm.

## 8. Smart questions to ask them

- How is the Vertical SaaS partner motion different in practice from the media-agency reseller motion — different playbooks, or one program applied to both?
- What does the partner summit look like today — size, cadence, who owns what — and how much of this role's first year is building toward it versus running steady-state programs?
- Is there an existing partner enablement library/asset base to build on, or is a chunk of the first 90 days building that foundation?
- How does this role split time between the established US/Europe base and the growing LATAM/APAC ecosystem?
- What does success look like at 90 days versus a year — pipeline/adoption metrics, or program/asset delivery milestones first?
- How much of the AI-tooling expectation in the JD is about using AI to do this job faster, versus positioning Duda's own AI features to partners? (Both are real strengths — worth knowing which one they weight more.)
