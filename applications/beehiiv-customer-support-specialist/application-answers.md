# Application Answers — Customer Support Specialist, beehiiv

## Tell us a little more about your experience with supporting a SaaS product. How much technical troubleshooting was incorporated in your roles?

Most of my SaaS career has been on the onboarding and implementation side, where troubleshooting isn't a separate function, it's most of the job. At Accelo, I sold and scoped CRM and project-management software to agencies and professional-services firms, then stayed on through onboarding: workflow design, API integrations, and resolving the technical issues that came up during setup, working alongside the implementation team rather than handing it off. At Wix, I guided every client through Google Tag Manager setup as part of launch. At Birdeye, I guided customers through configuration of reputation and marketing-automation tools directly, resolving issues myself rather than escalating them. Now at Solenzo, I build and debug the automation myself, so troubleshooting has gone from something I coordinate to something I do end to end.

## Describe something you built or improved with AI. What changed between your first attempt and the version you actually kept using?

At Solenzo, I run an AI-driven audit workflow that scores a business's digital footprint and generates personalized outreach, it's enrolled 180+ contacts to date. The version that runs today isn't the raw output of the model. I built rubric-based grading criteria that define what a passing output actually looks like, and I personally label a sample of outputs against that rubric before anything reaches a live account. The AI generates the first draft; the rubric and my own review are what decide whether it ships. That's the piece I'd point to as the real change: not a better prompt, but a quality gate I built and staffed myself so the system stays trustworthy at scale instead of just fast.
