## 1. What this interview likely is
micro1 vets domain *experts* who contribute high-quality input that trains/evaluates
frontier AI models. So expect the interview to test **depth of real marketing judgment**,
not buzzwords. Likely components (any combination):
- A **screening / domain interview** (sometimes AI-led) probing how you think about
  marketing strategy, branding, and storytelling.
- A **sample-of-work ask** — they may want to see an actual deck, or have you talk
  through how you'd structure one. **Have a real executive presentation ready to show
  or describe in detail.**
- A **scenario / mini-task** — e.g., "here's a brand and some data; how would you build
  the narrative?" Be ready to think out loud and structure a story live.

Framing to keep in mind: your "deliverable" as a contributor is *expert reasoning* an AI
can learn from. They reward people who can **explain the *why*** behind a strategy or a
slide, not just produce one.

## 4. The two gaps — handle them proactively and honestly
**A. "Expert PowerPoint / executive decks for major enterprises." — THIS IS YOUR STRENGTH.**
You've said you can build a deck better than most — so make this an asset you *show*, not
just claim. Plan:
- **Bring a real deck** (or two) to screen-share or talk through. Pick ones with a clear
  before/after or a decision they drove.
- **Talk about your process, not just the output** — this is what an AI-training company
  rewards. Be ready to articulate: how you find the *one* idea per slide, build a narrative
  arc (problem → insight → recommendation → ask), make data land visually, cut everything
  that doesn't drive the decision, and design for the specific audience in the room.
- **Have a point of view on craft** — e.g., why most corporate decks fail (too many ideas
  per slide, no narrative spine, decoration over clarity) and how you fix it. A strong
  opinion here separates you from people who just "know PowerPoint."
- If they give a live scenario ("here's a brand + data, build the story"), structure out
  loud: audience → the single takeaway → 3-beat arc → what each slide must prove.

**B. "Demonstrated success in Fortune 500 / large-scale companies."**
Truthful framing: you've *worked at* large, well-known tech companies (Wix — publicly
traded; Birdeye) and advised a broad range of B2B brands. Be honest that much of your
client work is SMB/mid-market, and position that as a strength: "I've owned the whole
strategy end-to-end, not just one slice — so I understand how the pieces connect."

## 5. Your differentiator — lean into it
micro1 is an AI-training company. You actually *build* with AI (Claude, ChatGPT, agentic
workflows). That's a real edge: you understand what high-quality, well-reasoned input looks
like to a model — which is exactly what they want contributors to produce. Say something
like: "I use these tools daily and build automated systems with them, so I have a feel for
what makes expert input genuinely useful for training a model versus generic filler."
(Note: the role says no AI experience required — so this is a bonus, not a crutch.)

## 6. Smart questions to ask them
- "What does a great expert contribution look like to you — is it the finished deck, or the
  reasoning and structure behind it?"
- "How is the work scoped — per project, per deck, ongoing? What does a typical engagement
  look like week to week?"
- "Who's the end audience for these presentations — your enterprise customers' teams, or
  internal model training, or both?"
- "How do you evaluate quality and give feedback to contributors?"
- "What separates your top contributors from the rest?"
