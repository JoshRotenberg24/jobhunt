# Follow Up

A follow-up that says "just checking in" asks the reader to do work and gives them
nothing. A follow-up that adds one useful thing is a second impression. Every message
this skill writes must earn its place in an inbox.

## Step 0 — Establish there is a channel at all

**Do this before writing a word.** Most application confirmations come from no-reply
addresses (`no-reply@us.greenhouse-mail.io`, `no-reply@ashbyhq.com`, Breezy, ADP). There
is nothing to reply to, and a follow-up with no delivery path is wasted work.

Check the tracker and the confirmation email for a reachable human. Then:

| Situation | Channel | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A named person emailed him | Reply in that thread | Write it |
| No-reply confirmation only | **LinkedIn** to a named recruiter or the function lead | Write a LinkedIn note, not an email |
| No-reply and no findable human | None | **Say so and do not write anything.** Recommend closing the row or spending the time on a role that has a human attached. |

When the channel is LinkedIn, constraints tighten: under 300 characters if it goes in a
connection request, no subject line, and it must survive being read on a phone.

**Never invent a recipient address.** Guessed patterns (`first@company.com`) read as spam
and cost more than the follow-up gains.

## Step 1 — Read the context

- `applications/tracker.md` for status, dates, and which trigger fired
- `applications/<slug>/match-report.md` for the role, the hiring problem, and the gaps
- `profile/master-profile.md` for Writing Standards (prose) and Verified Metrics
- `applications/<slug>/questions.md` if it exists, so nothing is repeated verbatim

If the situation is ambiguous, read the tracker rather than asking. The trigger table
resolves it.

## Step 2 — Pick the one new thing

**This is the whole skill.** Every message carries exactly one piece of value that was
not in the application. Pick from:

- A specific, non-obvious observation about their product, funnel, onboarding flow, or
  public content, made from actually looking at it
- A relevant thing that happened since applying (shipped work, a result at a client)
- A short, concrete answer to a gap the match report flagged, when that gap is
  probably the reason for silence
- A named question that shows he understands the actual problem of the role

Never: a restatement of enthusiasm, a summary of the resume, a request for an update
with nothing attached.

If there is genuinely nothing new to say, tell Josh that and recommend closing the loop
instead of sending a hollow nudge.

## Step 3 — Write it

**Hard constraints:**

- Under 100 words. Post-interview thank-you, under 80.
- No em dashes or en dashes. Commas, periods, parentheses.
- Subject line under 6 words, specific, no "Following up" or "Checking in"
- One clear close. A question he actually wants answered.

**Banned openings:** "I hope you're well", "I wanted to reach out", "I know you're
busy", "Just following up", "I recently came across", "I'm writing to inquire",
"Circling back", "Bumping this".

**Banned throughout:** "excited for the opportunity", "passionate about", "perfect fit",
"results-driven", "proven track record", "leveraged", "spearheaded", "synergy", "please
don't hesitate", "at your earliest convenience".

**Voice:** direct, commercially aware, no hedging, no apologizing for making contact.
Confident that the message is worth sending, because it is. It should read like a
competent operator wrote it in ninety seconds, not like a candidate performing.

## Step 4 — Output

For each message give: **timing** (when to send), **subject line**, **body**, and one
line naming **the new value it adds**. If the situation warrants two variants (direct
vs. warm), give two, not five.

## The six situations

| Situation | Timing | Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `applied` (silent) | 5 business days | One observation about their problem, then a single question. Do not ask about application status. |
| `applied` #2 (final) | 12 business days | Shorter than the first. States plainly it is the last note, leaves the door open, no guilt. |
| `thankyou` | Within 24 hours | Reference one specific thing discussed. Answer anything he answered weakly, in one sentence. No recap. |
| `screen` / `interview` (silent) | 7 days | Assume the process is slow, not dead. Add value, restate nothing. |
| `final` (silent) | 5 days, then 14 | First is light. At 14 days, ask directly for a decision timeline. Directness is appropriate this deep. |
| `close` | When the answer is clearly no | Thank them concretely, no bitterness, no re-pitch, one line leaving future contact open. This message exists to keep the relationship, not to reverse the decision. |

## Judgment rules

- **A follow-up cannot fix a fit problem.** If the match report shows a hard gap, say so
  to Josh rather than writing a message that will not move it.
- **Two follow-ups, then stop.** A third does damage. The tracker enforces this.
- **Never imply he has other offers unless he actually does.** Manufactured urgency is
  transparent and it reads as desperate, not scarce.
- **Do not send the same message to a recruiter and a hiring manager.** The recruiter
  cares about process and timeline. The hiring manager cares about the problem. Ask
  which one it is if the tracker does not say.
- **Never fabricate a reason for reaching out.** No invented mutual connections, no
  pretended news, no claimed encounters with their product that did not happen.
