## 1. ViewEngine — Client Success & Operations Manager
Digital marketing / SEO + AI-search-visibility agency · Remote · Apply by email (resume + video link)

### The actual application question (confirmed)
They ask for a **3-minute video** answering:
> "What is your approach to organization? How do you prioritize tasks when you have
> multiple clients with the same deadline?"
> (They say: explain your thinking, there's no right/wrong answer.)

### How to answer it — structure (system → prioritization → proof), ~3 min
1. **System first (30–40s).** "Nothing lives in my head." One source of truth — I run
   every client's goals, timelines, and deliverables in HubSpot/Notion, so status is
   always visible and I can re-plan fast when things shift.
2. **Prioritization when deadlines collide (60–75s).** I triage on three questions,
   out loud: (a) **Impact / risk** — which client relationship or renewal is most
   exposed if this slips? (b) **Dependencies** — what unblocks the production team or
   other work if I do it first? (c) **Effort-to-done** — clear the fast wins so the list
   shrinks. Then the non-negotiable: **communicate early.** If two things genuinely can't
   both land on time, I reset expectations *before* the deadline, not after — clients
   forgive a proactive heads-up, never a silent miss.
3. **Proof point (40s).** At Wix I was team lead and primary contact for a high-value,
   multi-vertical onboarding portfolio — overseeing scope, budgets, and delivery for a
   whole team of specialists — and the job was exactly this: many clients, overlapping
   launch deadlines, keeping every one on time to accelerate time-to-value.
4. **Close (10s).** "Organization isn't a tool for me, it's a promise to the client that
   I've got their timeline handled."

**Video delivery tips:** structure beats polish — hit the three beats above. Warm and
conversational, look into the camera, one real story (the Wix one). Practice once to land
under 3:00. A tidy background quietly reinforces the "organized" message.

### Likely interview questions to expect (SEO agency + client success + ops)
- **"Walk me through how you'd onboard a new SEO client."** → discovery of goals →
  baseline audit → 30/60/90 plan → set expectations that SEO compounds over months.
  (True ammo: technical SEO audits, keyword-gap & competitor analyses at Solenzo.)
- **"A client is frustrated their rankings haven't moved — how do you handle it?"** →
  expectation-setting on SEO's slow curve, show leading indicators (crawl health,
  impressions, keyword coverage) not just rankings, GA4/Search Console evidence.
- **"How do you explain technical SEO to a non-technical client?"** → translate to
  business outcomes; his whole profile is "technical translation."
- **Tools:** GA4, Google Search Console, Ahrefs, Semrush — all on his stack; say so.
- **AI search visibility** (their newer angle) → honest + curious: tie to his real
  agentic-AI / AI-driven systems work at Solenzo. Adjacent expertise, genuine interest.
- **The org/prioritization question again, live** — expect them to probe the video answer.

### Fit read
Strong-ish stretch (~68). Real SEO + client-success + ops overlap. Watch-out: they pitch
it as friendly to "career changers," which can signal a modest comp band — get the number
early.

## 2. RN Pad — Customer Onboarding / Success
Healthcare SaaS (nursing-assessment workflows for home-care / post-acute agencies) ·
Funded, profitable NYC startup · Fully remote, global team · Apply: send resume + LinkedIn
+ "anything else you'd like to share" — **no forms**

### The "actual question"
There's no screening question — the opening is **"anything else you'd like to share."**
That IS the prompt. Treat it as a 5–6 sentence intro note (the closest thing to a cover
letter). Draft:

> "I've spent 15+ years owning customer onboarding and success in SaaS — most directly at
> Wix and Birdeye, where my job was turning a complex product into fast, real value for
> non-technical users and keeping them for the long haul. The first 30–60 days is the part
> of the journey I care about most: building the onboarding plan, being the person they
> can count on after signup, and getting them to their first 'this works' moment quickly.
> RN Pad caught my attention because that outcome matters more when the end users are
> nurses and agencies with no time to waste on clunky software. I've also built operations
> inside a heavily regulated startup from scratch, so a compliance-sensitive healthcare
> context feels familiar, not foreign. I'd love to help your customers adopt faster and
> stick." (Then resume + LinkedIn.)

### Likely interview questions to expect (early healthcare SaaS · onboarding + support)
- **"Walk me through onboarding a new customer end-to-end."** → discovery → tailored
  onboarding plan → milestones to first value → handoff to ongoing success. (Ammo: "lead
  end-to-end onboarding, translating business requirements into technical config + hands-on
  training" — Solenzo/Wix.)
- **"How do you onboard non-technical users?"** → his sweet spot: onboarded SMBs at Wix,
  partners at Birdeye at high volume; reduce jargon, hands-on training, reusable guides.
- **"This role blends onboarding AND general support — how do you juggle both?"** →
  prioritization system (same muscle as the ViewEngine answer): protect onboarding
  milestones, triage support by impact, document so nothing drops.
- **"How do you measure onboarding success / reduce time-to-value?"** → activation
  milestones, adoption tracking, health metrics; he's tracked adoption/churn signals at
  Birdeye and built dashboards at Solenzo.
- **"Do you have healthcare experience?"** → honest bridge: no direct healthcare, but
  Standing Akimbo = built compliance + customer systems in a highly regulated startup;
  plus a decade onboarding non-technical users into new software. Curiosity > pretending.
- **"Comfortable with startup ambiguity / wearing many hats / async global team?"** →
  yes, and give the Solenzo founder evidence (builds systems from scratch, self-directed).

### Fit read
Solid stretch (~72). Cleanest confirmed no-form apply. Watch-outs: "general support
duties" and unstated comp/level — could sit below his level; confirm scope and pay early.
